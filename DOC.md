As we continue strengthening the digital presence of Mission to Seafarers across Canada, I would like the MTSSO website to better reflect how the Southern Ontario network is structured and make it much easier for seafarers, volunteers, donors, industry partners, and the general public to navigate.

The concept is for MTSSO to serve as the umbrella website for our Southern Ontario stations, with individual pages underneath it for:
• Toronto
• Hamilton
• Oshawa
• Port Colborne

The Toronto webpage has already been developed, so we would like to retain and migrate the work we have done there rather than start again.
Overall Website Structure

When someone arrives on the MTSSO website, I would like them to immediately understand three things:

1. Who MTSSO is.
The website should clearly explain Mission to Seafarers Southern Ontario and its relationship with Mission to Seafarers Canada and the wider global Mission.
2. Which stations fall under MTSSO.
Toronto, Hamilton, Oshawa, and Port Colborne should each be clearly visible and accessible from the main MTSSO website.

3. How visitors can engage.
A seafarer should be able to quickly find assistance in their port, while volunteers, donors, churches, industry partners, and community members should be able to easily find the information relevant to them.

MTSSO Homepage
The homepage should function as the umbrella rather than appearing to represent one particular station.
I would like it to include:
• A strong hero section introducing MTSSO
• A brief explanation of MTSSO and its relationship with Mission to Seafarers Canada
• A visual introduction to our four Southern Ontario stations
• An overview of what we do, including ship visits, transportation, hospitality, pastoral and emotional support, practical assistance, and connectivity
• Key impact numbers that we can update annually
• Recent stories from across the four stations
• Volunteer, partnership, and donation opportunities
• Clear information for seafarers looking for assistance

I would also like a simple visual that helps people understand the wider structure of the Mission:

The Mission to Seafarers Global → Mission to Seafarers Canada → MTSSO → Toronto, Hamilton, Oshawa and Port Colborne

We can confirm the appropriate wording around the organizational relationship before publishing.

Individual Station Pages
Rather than designing four completely separate websites, I think the most cost effective and manageable approach would be for you to develop one strong reusable station template.
We could then use that template for Toronto, Hamilton, Oshawa, and Port Colborne while allowing each station to have its own photographs, leadership, services, contact information, stories, and local information.
Each station page could include:
• Station introduction and hero image
• Information about the local port
• Meet the Chaplain or Station Team
• Services available to seafarers
• Ship visiting information
• Station address and contact information
• Transportation and local resources for seafarers
• Station history
• Photo gallery
• Latest news and stories from that station
• Volunteer opportunities
• Partnership opportunities
• Donate or support the station

The pages should be very mobile friendly because many seafarers will be accessing this information from their phones.



Central News and Story System
One feature I would particularly like is a central content management system rather than having to manually maintain separate news sections for every station.
For example, when we publish a story, we could tag it as:
Station: Toronto, Hamilton, Oshawa, Port Colborne, or MTSSO
and
Category: Ship Visits, Volunteers, Maritime News, Events, Seafarer Stories, Announcements, etc.
The website could then automatically display the appropriate stories on each station page while also feeding them into the main MTSSO News and Stories section.
That would make managing the website much easier for us long term.

For Seafarers
I would also like a central For Seafarers section that is simple and practical.
A visiting seafarer should be able to select their port and quickly find information about transportation, Wi Fi, SIM cards, shopping, pastoral support, station contacts, local resources, and emergency assistance.
This section should prioritize ease of use on mobile devices.
